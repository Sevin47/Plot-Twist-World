What is this?

Think Monopoly played on the actual Earth, with the rules of an idle game. You buy real-world parcels, they earn rent every second whether you're online or not, you build them up from an empty lot to a tower, and you trade — or fight — with real other players in one persistent, shared world. There's no fictional continent here: if you zoom in on your own street, it's really there, classified by whatever it actually is — downtown, waterfront, suburb, open water.

About ₲ Geobux ₲ Geobux is the game's currency. It's virtual and holds no real-world value — everything you earn, own and trade lives inside the game.
Did you know The map isn't a stylized approximation — every tile's classification is derived live from real OpenStreetMap water, land-use, and building-footprint data for that exact spot on Earth. Nobody hand-placed a single one; the game just reads what's really there.

Getting started

From opening the app for the first time to owning your first tile.

01
Press Play now — or sign in
You don't need an account to start. Play now drops you straight into a guest world in this browser. Signing in with Google instead gives you a permanent account that follows you across devices. Either way you get a real tile on the real map. See Playing as a guest.
02
Pick a username
2–16 letters, numbers, or underscores. Permanent for the account, and what other players see on your deeds, listings, messages, and the World register.
03
Choose your starting area
A basemap-only screen — pan and zoom the real map to find your home turf, then drop the pin.
04
Claim it & learn the ropes
Confirming rolls your first tile's rarity, claims it free, and sets that region as your permanent home. An eight-step interactive tutorial follows, covering the core loop live on your own deed — and new accounts start with a free rush credit, so your first build timer is on the house.

From there you're in the full game: the map, the six tabs along the bottom, and the whole economy described below.

The interface

Six tabs along the bottom, a persistent header, and a pause menu behind Menu at top-left.

Map
The world itself — pan, zoom, buy, build, attack, and watch the live feed of land changing hands.
Assets
Every tile you own, grouped under a collapsible header per region showing that region's tile count and combined rent. Home region opens by default, the rest start collapsed, biggest earner first. Searching or filtering opens every group. Tiles under construction sit in their own section up top, soonest-to-finish first.
Market
Player listings inside territory you've unlocked. See The open market.
Profile
You: status, achievements, your daily stipend, contracts and collections. A dot appears here whenever something is ready to claim.
World
Your territory and region unlocks, region projects, landmarks, and the recent activity log.
Social
Friends, direct messages, and your blocked list.

The header shows your balance, income rate, status rank, energy remaining, attacks remaining, and how many tiles are currently under construction — the whole account state at a glance, on every tab.

The pause menu Menu at top-left holds the things you don't need mid-session: the World register, this Wiki, Settings (reduce motion, alert toggles), Replay tutorial, Sign out, and Delete account & data.

Territory and fog

Once you own at least one tile, the interactive grid only lights up inside regions you've unlocked (roughly 150 km chunks of the world). Everywhere else appears fogged on the grid — you can still browse it at wide zoom, but you can't buy, attack, or trade there until you unlock that region. Your home region is always free. How a spot gets classified

  • If it's within roughly 150 m of open water, it becomes Waterfront (dense) or Coast (sparse).
  • Otherwise, real building-footprint density decides the tier: Downtown at ≥62% built-up coverage, Urban at ≥40%, Suburbs at ≥18%, Rural below that.
  • Explicit OpenStreetMap land-use tags (commercial, residential, industrial, park, farmland, etc.) override the density guess where they exist.
Surveying… If a spot hasn't finished loading real map data yet, it shows as "Surveying…" — not purchasable. This is honest uncertainty, not a guess. Give it a moment and it resolves to a real classification.
Did you know Price and rent scale together so tightly that classification barely affects your rent-per-₲-invested — Downtown edges out the rest at 0.281 rps per ₲1,000, Suburbs trails at 0.25. The real choice is how much capital you want tied up in one tile, not which classification is "best." See Starter strategies.

Rarity

Every tile is rolled a rarity the moment you buy it — pure luck, not something you can choose or influence.

RarityOddsRent multiplier
Common70%×1
Uncommon20%×1.5
Rare8%×3
Legendary2%×8

Rarity applies for as long as you own that specific tile and multiplies its rent directly. Selling a tile or losing it in combat loses the rarity roll with it — whoever ends up owning it next (via purchase, listing, or capture) keeps whatever rarity is already on the tile; only a brand-new claim on previously-unowned land rolls fresh. Landmark tiles never roll rarity — they're always Common, on purpose, so the incentive to hold one is the perk and the trophy, not a lucky multiplier.

Buying land & energy

Claiming a brand-new, never-owned tile is gated by energy — a daily allowance, not a wallet check.

Energy is the real limiter on how fast the map gets claimed — not how much ₲ you have. Every claim of a new, unowned tile costs 1 energy. Trading with other players and upgrading tiles you already own never cost energy.

Hard daily cap — no banking Your daily energy is set by your current status rank, and arrives in two halves — at 00:00 and 12:00 UTC. Unused energy does not carry over to the next day: a day you don't spend it is a day it's gone, not saved up.

The split is about when, not how much — the daily total is exactly the same as if it all landed at once. Any half you haven't collected yet is handed over the moment you next open the game, so a single visit at 20:00 gets you the full day's energy; you only need two visits if you want to spend the first half early and still have the rest available later.

Claiming your very first tile ever (via the starting-area picker) is always free and unrestricted — no region-unlock needed, no energy spent. Every claim after that follows the normal rules.

A tile starts as a vacant lot and can be built up through four levels — and building takes real, elapsed time.

Rent scales directly with level: a Tower earns 5× the tile's base rent (level counts as 1 + level). Starting an upgrade begins a real countdown — the tile keeps earning its current rent while it builds, then jumps to the new rent the moment it finishes.

Builder slots

How many tiles can be under construction at once is capped by your status rank — 2 slots at Squatter, rising to 6 at Mogul — plus any permanent slots from collections (up to +5 more). Starting an upgrade while all your slots are full simply isn't possible until one finishes.

Rushing a build

Impatient? Rushing pays the remaining fraction of the full upgrade cost to finish instantly — rush a build that's 90% done and you're only paying for the last 10%, not the whole thing again. Rushing is the game's main money sink, and both a landmark perk and the Contractors' Guild project discount it. See Formula reference for the exact math.

Upgrade All & Rush all

From the Assets tab, Upgrade All starts a build on every eligible, affordable tile (cheapest first) until your builder slots or balance run out.

Rush all, on the Building section, finishes every build you have running, one after another, and shows the total cost before you commit. If you're holding rush credits it spends them on your most expensive builds first, so a credit is never burned on a five-minute Cottage; once they're gone it works up from the cheapest, finishing as many as your ₲ allows.

Anything either button starts or finishes appears in the dedicated Building section at the top of Assets, so you can see exactly what it did.

Naming your tiles

Give any tile you own a private nickname — useful once your portfolio grows past a handful of coordinates that all start to look alike.

Tap the ✎ next to a tile's name in its sheet to set one, up to 24 characters. It's private to you — nobody else, including whoever owned the tile before you or buys/captures it after you, ever sees it. It also shows up in the Assets tab in place of the raw coordinates, and Assets search matches nicknames too.

Resets on transfer A nickname is cleared automatically the moment a tile changes hands — sold on the market, captured in a raid, or repossessed for inactivity. The next owner starts with a blank slate to name it themselves, the same as you did.

Every tile also has a short, permanent, non-editable ID (the last 6 characters of its map coordinate string) shown in both the sheet and the Assets list — two nearby tiles can round to the same displayed coordinates, or even end up with the same nickname, so this ID is what actually guarantees you're looking at the right one.

Redeveloping (prestige)

Once a tile is maxed out at Tower, tear it down for a permanent bonus instead of just sitting on it.

Redevelop resets a Tower back to a Vacant lot — instantly, for free — and grants a permanent +25% rent bonus on that tile, stacking with every previous cycle. There's no hard limit on how many times you can redevelop the same tile.

Redevelop is free — rebuilding isn't The reset itself costs nothing. What gets more expensive is climbing back to Tower afterward: each redevelop cycle raises both the ₲ cost and the time to rebuild through all four levels again. That penalty caps out after 10 cycles — the rent bonus keeps growing forever, but cost and build time stop climbing.

Redevelop keeps the tile — you still own it, you're just resetting the building to earn a permanent multiplier. There is no way to cash a tile out for a lump sum in the current version; see the note in The open market.

Redevelop is also the covenant moment The first time you redevelop a given tile, you're offered three covenants — permanent two-sided terms on that one tile. It's a once-per-tile offer and declining it is final, so it's worth reading before you tap. A covenant you sign survives every later redevelop; a tile that already carries one is never offered another.

A covenant can also change when you're allowed to redevelop: Heritage Listing and Ghost Tenancy cap the tile at Apartments, so it becomes redevelopable a level early — a faster, cheaper prestige loop that skips the 8-hour Tower build. Freehold, Holdout Parcel and Sovereign Charter go the other way and block redeveloping entirely.

Covenants

Permanent, two-sided terms on a single tile — the one system in the game that can make a specific tile worse in exchange for making it better at something else.

Rent is otherwise a straight sum over everything you own, which means owning more is unconditionally better and no purchase is ever really a decision. Covenants are the answer to that: each one is a trade, and each one is bad for some plan you might have had.

How you get one

  • Offered on redevelop, and only on redevelop — three cards, drawn at random, in the tile's own sheet. Nothing interrupts you; the offer waits, and a violet dot on Assets counts how many tiles have one pending.
  • Once per tile, per owner. A tile that already carries a covenant is never offered another, and neither is one whose owner turned an offer down.
  • Declining is free but final. You can take one or none — but "none" is itself the permanent answer for as long as you hold that tile. It asks twice before it commits.
Why declining is permanent It didn't used to be, and that was a mistake: declining cost nothing and the next redevelop dealt three fresh cards, so the correct play was to keep re-rolling until a no-downside card appeared. That made every card with a real cost worthless and turned a decision into a grind. Now the refusal is the decision, and both sides of it are real.

How long one lasts

A covenant is permanent for your tenure on that tile. It survives every rebuild and every redevelop cycle, forever, for as long as the tile is yours. It ends the moment the tile stops being yours — sold on the market, captured in a raid, abandoned for the refund, or repossessed for inactivity. The next owner gets clean ground and their own one-time offer.

A covenant is public while it's in force — a rival's tile shows its terms on the sheet before you raid it, which is information you want. But market listings show nothing, because a buyer never ends up under the seller's terms: the covenant is cleared on transfer either way. Listing a tile doesn't clear or suspend it; the terms hold until the sale actually completes.

Did you know The Black Ledger is the trap on purpose. 2.5× is the largest rent number in the game and it lasts a month — and then the tile is a half-rent millstone for as long as you hold it, with no exit but giving the tile up. Abandoning a tile

Give up any tile you own instantly for a partial refund.

Abandoning refunds 50% of everything you've paid into that tile — purchase price plus every upgrade — and releases it back to the world, unowned. Quick, simple, always available. Useful for cutting losses on a tile that isn't earning its keep, or one that's become a PvP liability.

Region projects

Shared infrastructure that a whole region builds together — funded by a cut of what everyone there already spends, not by a new charge.

Every region has a fund. 10% of what you spend claiming, building and rushing in that region is carved out of the existing price and goes to the fund instead of disappearing. Same prices, same buttons — the levy is a redirection, not a surcharge. When the fund reaches a project's cost, the project is built and everyone holding land in that region benefits, you included.

Upkeep and pausing

A built project keeps running for as long as the fund can pay its upkeep. If a region's players drift away, the fund stops keeping up and its projects pause until it recovers — nothing is lost and no progress is destroyed, it just waits. A busy region sustains two projects comfortably; a near-empty one won't sustain one.

Choosing what gets built

You can back a specific project with your region's fund, or leave it alone and the fund follows whatever the region is closest to finishing. In a region where you're the only landlord, that makes the choice entirely yours. In a busy one, nobody has to organise anything: every landlord who doesn't pick is quietly pushing whichever project is already furthest along, so the region converges on one instead of leaving four stuck at a quarter each. Backing a project is a standing choice, not a vote — change it whenever you like and your future levy redirects, but ₲ already in a bar stays there.

Did you know The 5% market duty on sales (see The open market) is the only genuinely new cost that arrived with projects — everything else was already being spent. It exists so Free Port has something to waive, which gives a trading-heavy region a project worth arguing for over Metro Line.

Landmarks

24 real-world landmarks, 9 tiles each (216 total worldwide) — expensive, rarity-free, and the source of the game's only non-income perks.

LandmarksPerkValue per tileCap
Eiffel Tower, Sagrada Família, Neuschwanstein, Tokyo Tower, CN Tower, Space NeedleBuild speed−2% build time30%
Great Wall, Colosseum, Kremlin, Petra, Machu Picchu, Angkor WatSiege discount−2% attack cost30%
Statue of Liberty, Golden Gate Bridge, Burj Khalifa, Mount Rushmore, Table Mountain, AcropolisEnergy boost+2 energy/daynone
Big Ben, Taj Mahal, Great Pyramid, Sydney Opera House, Christ the Redeemer, Leaning Tower of PisaRush discount−2% rush cost30%
  • Claim price: a flat ₲1,000,000 per tile, regardless of which landmark or real-world classification it would otherwise have.
  • No rarity roll — always Common. The reward is the perk and the trophy, not a lucky multiplier.
  • Defense multiplier: ×1.5 defense power against attacks — landmark tiles are meaningfully harder to take than an ordinary tile of the same level.
  • 48-hour grace period: a landmark tile can't be attacked for 48 hours after it changes owner (by any means), so a fresh claim isn't immediately sieged back.
  • Perks are region-scoped (build speed, siege discount, rush discount only count landmark tiles you own in the same region as the tile being upgraded/attacked/rushed) — except energy boost, which is account-wide, since energy itself isn't tied to any one region.

Perk value stacks per tile owned — own all 9 tiles of one landmark and a percentage-based perk reaches 18% (under its 30% cap); an energy-boost landmark's full set adds a flat +18 energy/day.

PvP & attacks

Take a tile from another player by force — but only from your own border.

Adjacency

You can only attack a tile if you already own at least one tile orthogonally adjacent to it (north, south, east, or west — not diagonal). This keeps PvP a contiguous frontier, not a random snipe from across the planet.

Daily limits

Attacks you may launch per day scale with your status rank — 6/day at Squatter, up to 20/day at Mogul (see the status table for every rank in between). Attacks a single tile may absorb per day is flat for everyone: 2, regardless of either side's rank.

The two caps are independent — a well-defended tile can only be hit twice a day regardless of who's attacking it, but your daily attack allowance lets you try elsewhere the moment one target is tapped out, rather than losing the rest of your day.

Cost & odds

Attack cost is the greater of a base formula (half the target's price, scaling with its level) or 0.2% of your own peak net worth — whichever is higher. See Formula reference for the exact numbers. Win probability is your attack power (how many owned tiles border the target) versus the defender's power (their level, rarity, prestige, and any landmark defense bonus), clamped between 5% and 90% either way — no attack is ever a sure thing, and no defense is ever unbeatable.

Losing costs nothing extra A failed attack still costs the attack fee — that's the whole risk. A successful attack transfers the tile outright, resetting any build in progress; a successful defense costs the defender nothing and keeps any build running.
Did you know The wealth floor (0.2% of peak net worth) means a brand-new account's attacks are priced almost entirely by the base formula — the floor only starts to bite once your peak net worth climbs into real money. Punching up is always cheap; punching down gets expensive as you get rich.

What the defender can change

Defense isn't only level and rarity. Before you commit to a target, check what's on it — a rival's covenant is public, and it's printed on the tile's sheet:

  • Untargetable outright — Conservation Easement, Holdout Parcel and Sovereign Charter mean the tile can never be attacked by anyone, at any cost.
  • Defense multipliers — Iron Covenant (×4), Fortified Block (×2), Bunker Lot (+75%), Gated Community (+50%), Civic Trust (+25%) … or the other way: Midas Clause (quarter), Flood Plain (half), Anchor Tenant (−40%), Company Town (−30%).
  • Attack cost — Gated Community doubles what you pay and Iron Covenant triples it, while Night Market and Midas Clause halve it. A high-rent tile with cheap raiding on it is the softest target in the game.
  • Region projects — Civic Fortifications adds 35% defense to every tile in the region, including tiles with no covenant at all.

Winning a raid and capturing a tile unlocks the Conqueror achievement. New players and accounts less than 72 hours old can't be attacked at all. Capturing a tile clears whatever covenant was on it — that was the loser's bargain, and the winner takes clean ground.

Company landlords

Eight holding companies buy up quiet parts of the map, so there's always a neighbour to raid and trade against — and they withdraw as real landlords arrive.

A company landlord is an ordinary landlord in every mechanical sense: it owns real tiles, its name shows on them, it borders you, you can attack it, and you can buy from it on the market. The only difference is that there's no person behind it. They exist because attacks, borders and trading all assume someone is nearby, and in an empty stretch of map all three quietly stop working.

The companies
Bolder Boulders Holdings · Artisanal Gravel Estates · InvisiBlinds Property Co · Soup Loans Realty · Pigeon Mail Land Trust · The Ladder Store Group · Mildly Haunted Realty · Actual Cloud Holdings

Where they move in

A company will only claim a tile in a neighbourhood where no real landlord owns anything. A neighbourhood here is roughly 9.8km across — the same kind of square as a region, just much smaller. They're capped at 4 tiles per neighbourhood and 24 per region, and they buy at most one new tile per region every two hours, so a quiet area fills in gradually rather than all at once.

The practical effect: companies fill in around you, never on top of you. They can't appear in a neighbourhood you've already claimed anything in.

Where they move out

Once real landlords start buying in a neighbourhood a company holds tiles in, that company begins withdrawing from it:

  • First it lists the tile on the open market at 80% of what it paid — so a company pulling out is a source of cheap land for whoever moved in, not an obstruction.
  • Then it releases the tile back to unowned if nobody buys it within 48 hours of listing.
  • Never within 24 hours of the company acquiring the tile, so land can't dissolve out from under you mid-campaign.
  • Never below 3 tiles per region — a small company presence always survives, so there's a practice target available even in a busy area.

Attacking them

Exactly the same rules as attacking a player: you need an orthogonally adjacent tile, it costs the same, it counts against the same daily caps, and a single company tile still only absorbs 2 attacks a day. Their tiles are seeded partly built (level 1–3, usually Common) so they're worth taking, and a captured tile resets to Vacant/Common like any other capture.

They get harder the more you take Every tile you capture from a given company raises the defense power of that company's remaining tiles by 35% — against you specifically. Take four tiles off Soup Loans Realty and its fifth is more than twice as hard for you as its first was. Other landlords are unaffected: a company never gets harder for a newcomer because someone else has been grinding it.

The escalation is per company, so spreading your attacks across several of them stays cheaper than farming one to the ground. It resets for nobody — it's a permanent record of what you've taken.

What they never do

  • They never attack you. Losing a tile while you were offline to something that isn't even a person is not a fight, it's just a tax.
  • They never claim landmark tiles. Those are meant to be contested between real landlords.
  • They don't appear on the World register, and can't be added as friends or messaged — they're companies, not players.
Did you know Unlike a real landlord, a company is never protected by the 72-hour new-account shield — a company that moved in an hour ago is fair game immediately. It also never decays from inactivity the way an absent player's tiles do, because "inactive" is its permanent condition; withdrawal is handled entirely by the neighbourhood rules above.

The open market

A live, player-driven trading post.

Any tile you own can be listed at any price you choose. Sales pay you instantly, even while you're offline — the buyer gets exactly what you listed: your level, rarity, and prestige, unchanged.

5% duty on sales Selling costs the seller a 5% duty on the proceeds. It's the one genuinely new charge that arrived with region projects — and a region that has built Free Port waives it entirely on sales between landlords there, which is what makes that project worth arguing for.
Only your unlocked territory The Market only shows — and only lets you buy — tiles inside regions you've already unlocked, the same boundary the map's fog draws for browsing. Unlock a new region to trade there too.

Listings deliberately say nothing about covenants, because a buyer never inherits one: a covenant belongs to the seller's tenure and is cleared the moment the tile transfers. What you're buying is clean ground plus whatever level, rarity and prestige is on it, and you draft your own terms on your first redevelop. Some covenants (Mixed Use, Company Town, Co-op Board, Founder's Pact) forbid listing altogether; abandoning still works on those.

Selling is a way off a covenant Since the terms end with your tenure, selling a tile is a legitimate exit from a covenant you regret — an expired Ground Lease or a spent Black Ledger is worth exactly what a buyer will pay for a clean tile at that level. Listing alone doesn't clear anything: the covenant stays in force, with its rent and defense effects, right up until the sale completes, and pulling the listing leaves it exactly where it was.
Removed feature An earlier version of the game let players "flip" a maxed-out tile for an instant, formula-priced payout instead of listing it. It was cut — Abandoning already covers "I want out now," and listing already covers "I want to sell on my own terms," so flip didn't carry its own weight as a third option. If you're reading an older guide or screenshot that mentions flipping, it no longer exists.

Sticky — it never drops Status is based on the highest net worth your account has ever reached, not your current one. Spending big, a bad trade, or losing a tile in combat will never demote you. Once earned, a rank is yours forever.

Net worth here means your balance plus everything you've invested across every tile you own — the same number the leaderboard ranks by, and the same number the PvP wealth floor is based on.

Reaching a new rank announces itself. A promotion opens with the rank you just made and every number it raised, shown as the change rather than the total — daily energy, builder slots, attacks a day, and a note when contracts your account couldn't previously be handed have joined the rotation. It can arrive up to about 20 seconds after the purchase that earned it: your peak net worth is recalculated on the server, and the game only learns about it on its next sync.

Did you know Daily attacks scale with status now, not just energy — a Squatter and a Mogul used to get the exact same 6 attacks a day despite everything else about their accounts being wildly different. Mogul's 250,000,000 threshold is set well past what even a serious landmark collector needs (216 tiles world-wide at ₲1,000,000 each) — the ladder is meant to keep giving you somewhere to climb.

World register

The top 10 landlords by current net worth (balance + total invested across all owned tiles) — on the pause menu, the way most games put their leaderboard on the title screen.

Unlike status rank, the register reflects your current standing, not your peak — it moves up or down as players buy, sell, and build. Your status rank badge shows next to your name, every name on it is tappable for that landlord's stats card, and company landlords never appear on it.

Live feed

A running list on the map of every tile that changes hands anywhere in the world, the moment it happens.

Claimed, bought off the market, or captured in a raid — each entry shows who took it, what kind of land it was, and what it cost them. Your own show up as "You". Tap an entry to fly straight to that tile.

Fold the panel away with its "Live feed" header and it stays folded next time, showing a count of what you missed while it was closed.

What it deliberately leaves out Company landlords' land grabs stay out of the feed, and so do attacks that were repelled — a successfully defended tile never changed hands, so reporting it would be noise. Only real transfers between real landlords appear.

World land claimed

A live counter on the map showing how much of the world's purchasable land has been claimed, worldwide, by everyone.

The denominator isn't a guess — it's computed from real coastline data (Natural Earth's 1:50m land polygons), reprojected into the exact same map projection the game's tile grid uses and clipped to the same reachable latitude band, so it accounts for how that projection distorts area at different latitudes. The result: about 38.35% of the reachable grid is land, versus the ~29.2% you'd get from Earth's real spherical surface area — projected map space and real surface area aren't the same thing, and using the wrong one would have undercounted by nearly a third.

Did you know Almost all of Antarctica sits outside the game's coordinate system entirely — the map projection is clipped at about ±85.05° latitude (the same limit real-world slippy maps use), so there's no tile coordinate for most of the deep south. It isn't excluded on purpose; there's simply nowhere to put it.

Friends

Add other players by username from the Social tab. Costs nothing, and their territory lights up teal on the map.

Send a request by exact landlord name; they can accept, decline, or block. Sending each other a request at the same time auto-accepts instead of creating two pending requests. From your friends list you can jump straight to a friend's home region with one tap. Making your first friend unlocks the Sociable achievement.

Rate limit Up to 20 pending friend requests may be sent per day.

Direct messages

Private, friends-only conversations — and the game's only channel. You can't be messaged by anyone you haven't accepted, and there's no open or regional room to be pulled into.

Open a conversation from any friend's row on the Social tab. Unread counts show per-friend and as a dot on that tab. Sending a first message unlocks the Pen pal achievement.

Limits Up to 20 messages per minute, per account. Each conversation keeps its most recent 200 messages.

Blocking

There is no public or open channel anywhere in the game — the only way anyone can message you is by being a friend you accepted. Blocking is how you make sure someone never becomes one.

Block sits next to Accept and Decline on any incoming friend request, on the Social tab. It's mutual and immediate: a blocked player can't send you requests or messages, and you can't reach them either. Nothing is announced — they aren't told, and blocked players only ever appear on your list.

Blocks last until you lift them. Only you can, from the Blocked list under the Social tab's friends, and unblocking resets the two of you to strangers rather than restoring anything. If someone's already a friend and you'd rather they weren't, Remove on their row ends the friendship — and with it the ability to message each other at all.

Playing as a guest

You can play without an account. Press Play now, pick your spot, and the map is yours — no sign-in, no email, nothing to fill in first.

A guest world is not a demo. Your tile is a real tile on the real map, with a real district, a real rarity roll, and real rent accruing while you're away. It sits in the same world everyone else is playing in.

Signing in with Google whenever you're ready brings everything with you — same tile, same ₲, same streak, now on every device you sign into. Doing it earns +₲1,000 and a free rush.

One catch worth knowing A guest world lives in that browser alone. There's no account to sign back into, so clearing your history or site data before you sign in means it's gone — permanently, with nothing recoverable. If you're enjoying it, sign in.

Tutorial & tips

An eight-step interactive walkthrough for brand-new accounts, plus one-shot tips that appear the first time something actually matters.

The tutorial starts automatically right after your first claim, once the welcome animations settle, and covers the core loop live on your own tile. Every step dims the screen around the real thing it's talking about — the actual button, counter or tile, including your own land on the map, which stays lit while you pan and zoom.

Steps that teach an action wait for you to do it rather than offering a Next button; sit on one for 20 seconds and it offers to skip itself. "Skip tour" is always available, the tour ends on your portfolio rather than the map, and finishing it on one device counts on all of them. Replay it any time from the pause menu. Completing it unlocks the Oriented achievement.

Tips

Separate from the tour, short tips fire once each the first time they're relevant: rent collecting itself, unlocking territory, running out of energy, rewards waiting to be claimed, your first raid, what redeveloping does (one level before you can do it), and the covenant offer. Any tile you can still build on also says what happens at Tower, so redeveloping isn't something you find by accident.

Daily streak

Open the game once per day (UTC) to build a streak, then collect a stipend that grows with it.

Daily reward₲150 × min(current streak, 7)

The reward climbs each day through day 7 (₲1,050), then holds flat at ₲1,050/day for every day after — a day-100 streak pays the same as a day-7 streak. Missing a single day resets your streak back to 1. A 3-day streak unlocks the Regular achievement.

Showing up and collecting are two different things. Your streak advances the moment you open the game, whether or not you go and get the money — so a session spent entirely on the map never costs you a day. The stipend itself is a card at the top of the Profile tab, showing the seven-day ladder, what today pays and what tomorrow pays if you come back. Tap Collect to take it; the dot on the Profile tab is there until you do.

Unlike a collection, an uncollected stipend does not wait: it expires at 00:00 UTC, and the card counts down to it. Missing the money doesn't break the streak, though — that was already banked when you opened the game.

Offline earnings

Rent keeps accruing while you're away, at two different rates depending on exactly how long it's been.

Actively synced (≤2 min)
Full rent rate. A boost's 2× multiplier only applies inside this window.
Away (>2 min)
Half rate, capped at 8 hours worth — a week-long trip earns the same as an 8-hour gap, not 20× more.
Did you know Boosts don't apply retroactively to offline time — the 2× multiplier only counts while you were actively syncing (within 2 minutes of your last check-in). Popping a boost right before closing the tab doesn't boost the hours you're away.

Notifications

Two opt-in push alerts, both off by default, both toggled from Settings on the pause menu.

AlertWhen it fires

Nothing is time-sensitive These exist so you can choose to be told, not because the game punishes you for missing a window. Unclaimed energy halves are handed over whenever you next open the game, finished contracts pay out whether or not you were there, and a captured tile is reported in your activity log either way.

Boosts

Watch a short spot for a temporary rent multiplier.

Tapping the boost button plays a short promo and grants 2× rent for 5 minutes, only while actively playing (see Offline earnings). It's on a 30-minute cooldown between uses — long enough to be something to catch, not a timer to babysit.

Contracts

Three daily jobs and one weekly one, on your Profile tab. They're finished by playing normally — and they pay in things ₲ can't buy.

You hold 3 daily contracts and 1 weekly contract at a time, drawn at random from a pool of 26. Progress is credited automatically as you play: claim a tile, finish a build, win a raid, sell something, and whichever contracts cover that just went up. There's nothing to activate or accept.

Daily contracts rotate at 00:00 UTC, the same reset as your energy. The weekly one rotates on Monday. A dot appears on the Profile tab whenever something is ready to claim.

What they pay

Contract rewards lean deliberately toward things you can't just buy:

  • ₲ — modest amounts. Useful early, less interesting once your rent compounds.
  • Bonus energy — granted on top of your daily cap, so a contract can take you above your normal ceiling for the day. Like all energy, it doesn't carry into tomorrow.
  • Rush credits — the interesting one. A rush credit finishes any in-progress build instantly and free, regardless of what rushing it would have cost. They're spent automatically the next time you rush, and the Rush button reads "free" while you're holding any.
Did you know A rush credit is worth more the more expensive the build — rushing a fresh Tower costs far more than a fresh Cottage, but one credit covers either. Saving your credits for high-level builds is strictly better than burning them on Cottages, which is the whole reason contracts pay in credits rather than in the ₲ those rushes would have cost.

Nothing is wasted

Finished contracts always pay If you complete a contract but don't open the game to claim it before it rotates out, it pays automatically anyway — the rewards land on your account and show up in your "while you were away" summary and your activity log. Expiring something you actually earned because you didn't log in at the right hour would make the whole thing an obligation, which is exactly what it isn't.

Contracts you haven't finished simply rotate away with no penalty. There's no streak to protect and nothing to lose by ignoring them entirely.

Collections

Thirteen sets that reward you for which tiles you own rather than how many — and pay permanently.

Every other system in the game treats a rural tile in Mongolia and a rural tile in Kansas as the same object. Collections are where that stops: they're the reason to care about a tile's district, its neighbours, and where on Earth it actually sits. Progress shows on your Profile tab and is worked out live from whatever you currently own — there's nothing to opt into.

What they pay

Collection rewards are permanent capacity, never rent:

  • +daily energy — a permanent raise to your daily allowance, stacking on top of your status rank and any landmark energy boost.
  • +builder slots — a permanent extra concurrent build.
  • Titles, for four of the harder ones.

Completing all thirteen is worth +10 daily energy and +5 builder slots, forever — comparable to several status ranks' worth of capacity, earned by portfolio shape instead of net worth.

Did you know Collections deliberately don't boost rent, unlike the equivalent feature in most games of this kind. A rent bonus is a win-more engine — it pays out most to whoever is already furthest ahead. Capacity rewards give you more to do instead of more to bank, which is the same rule landmark perks are built on.

Blocks, and why they're exact

Block Party and City Block ask for a genuinely contiguous square — 4 tiles in a 2×2, or 16 in a 4×4 — not just four tiles near each other. The progress number shows your best single block, so 3/4 means you're one tile away from completing some specific square, and it's worth checking which.

These are the only collections where which particular tiles you own matters down to the individual parcel, and they're the reason it can be worth buying a tile you'd otherwise skip.

Claiming

Claimed is claimed Progress is recalculated live from the tiles you currently hold, so selling, abandoning, or losing a tile can un-finish a set you haven't claimed yet. It can never take back one you already claimed — the energy, the slots and the title are yours permanently, whatever happens to the portfolio afterwards.

The practical advice: claim a collection the moment it lights up. There's no reason to wait, and holding a completed set unclaimed is the only way to lose one.

Inactivity & repossession

If an account goes untouched for 30+ days, its tiles are gradually returned to the world a few at a time, with a 30% refund credited to that account and a notification waiting on next login. This keeps abandoned land from sitting locked forever in a world where every tile is one-of-one — it's not a punishment, just how a shared, finite world stays healthy. Repossessed tiles go back to genuinely unclaimed status — anyone can buy them fresh, no adjacency or region-unlock shortcuts.

Three covenants make a tile immune to this entirely: Freehold, Co-op Board and the rare Sovereign Charter. A tile carrying one of those is never repossessed no matter how long the account sits idle — which is most of what you're buying with Freehold's inability to be redeveloped.

Starter strategies

A few different viable approaches to the early game, grounded in the actual formulas above rather than folk wisdom. None of these are mandatory or mutually exclusive.

The Daily GrindBest for: everyone
Energy doesn't bank and a missed day resets your streak all the way to 1. The single highest-value habit in the game is logging in every day, spending your energy the same day, and tapping Collect on the stipend card in Profile — it expires at 00:00 UTC even though the streak behind it doesn't. This alone outperforms most "clever" plays over a long enough timeline. Since energy now lands in two halves (00:00 and 12:00 UTC), checking in on both sides of noon lets you spend the first half immediately instead of sitting on it — but a single later visit still collects the whole day's worth.
Rural SpamBest for: brand-new accounts
At ₲50 a tile, a Squatter's 10 daily energy can claim 10 Rural tiles for ₲500 total — comfortably inside most starting balances. Spreading your first day's energy across many cheap footholds compounds rent immediately, instead of saving toward one Downtown tile and leaving most of that day's energy unspent.
Classification Doesn't Matter (Yet)Best for: capital allocation
Rent-per-₲-invested is nearly flat across classifications (0.25–0.28 per ₲1,000 — see Property classification), and upgrade cost scales off the same base price, so the real decision isn't "which classification" but "how much capital do I want locked in one tile." Many cheap tiles spread your exposure to a bad PvP day across more surface area; one expensive tile concentrates it.
Punch Up While You're SmallBest for: early-to-mid PvP
Attack cost is whichever is higher: the base formula, or 0.2% of your peak net worth. A new account's peak net worth is near zero, so that floor barely applies yet — your attacks are about as cheap as they'll ever be. As your net worth grows, so does the floor, and PvP gets structurally more expensive. If raiding appeals to you at all, it's cheapest before you're established, not after.
Treat Landmarks as a Late-Game ProjectBest for: established accounts
A flat ₲1,000,000 per tile with no rarity roll and a 48-hour attack grace period is not a starter buy. Landmark perks are account-shaping utility (build speed, cheaper sieges, cheaper rushes, more energy), not raw income, and they only get meaningful once you own several tiles of the same landmark. Save them for once your economy can absorb a seven-figure purchase without blinking.
Decide Your Covenant Policy Before You RedevelopBest for: anyone reaching Tower
The covenant offer comes once per tile and declining is permanent, so "I'll see what shows up next cycle" isn't a plan any more. Work out what the tile is for first. A deep-interior tile nobody can reach is a rent tile — Transit Hub, Flood Plain and Company Town hand you 35–45% more rent for defense you were never going to need. A frontier tile with hostile neighbours wants Gated Community or Fortified Block, and the rent cost is the price of keeping it. A tile you intend to sell should stay clean, or at least avoid the never-listable cards. Signing nothing is a perfectly respectable answer for a tile you have no plan for.
Concentrate to Build ProjectsBest for: mid-game
A region project costs ₲120,000 from the region fund, filled by 10% of what everyone spends there. In a region where you're the only serious landlord, that fund is effectively your own money coming back as infrastructure — so spreading thinly across five regions means five funds that never reach ₲120,000, while concentrating means Metro Line and Contractors' Guild paying you back on every build and rush from then on. Upkeep scales with how many tiles you hold there, so this rewards depth over sprawl.
Make Friends on Day OneBest for: everyone, costs nothing
Friends cost nothing to add, light up teal on the map so you can see their footprint from orbit, unlock DMs, and let you jump to their home region with one tap. Friendship is also the only channel in the game — there's nowhere else to talk to a neighbour, so if you want to coordinate on a region project or agree not to raid each other, adding them is the move. There's no direct mechanical bonus, but a persistent shared world is a lot stickier with people you actually know in it — and it's the cheapest strategy on this list.

Formula reference

Every formula that drives the economy, exactly as the game computes it. l = building level (0–4), r = rarity multiplier, pr = prestige cycles, price = the tile's base classification price.

Rent per second

rent = base_rps × rarity_mult × (1 + level) × (1 + 0.25 × prestige)

Upgrade cost

cost = round( price × 0.8 × (level + 1)1.6 × (1 + 0.5 × min(prestige, 10)) )

Build duration

seconds = base_seconds[target level] × (1 + 0.25 × min(prestige, 10)) × (1 − landmark_build_speed% / 100) base_seconds: Lv1 = 300 (5 min) · Lv2 = 1,800 (30 min) · Lv3 = 7,200 (2 h) · Lv4 = 28,800 (8 h)

Rush cost

cost = ceil( upgrade_cost × (seconds_remaining / total_seconds) × (1 − landmark_rush_discount% / 100) )

Redevelop

level → 0, prestige → prestige + 1 (no direct ₲ cost)

Region unlock cost

cost = 1,000 × 2 ^ (paid regions already unlocked)

Attack cost

cost = max( round( target_price × 0.5 × (1 + 0.5 × target_level) × (1 − landmark_siege_discount% / 100) ), round( attacker_peak_net_worth × 0.002 ) )

Defense power

power = (1 + level) × rarity_mult × (1 + 0.25 × prestige) × landmark_defense_mult (×1.5 if applicable)

Attack power & win chance

attack_power = number of tiles you own orthogonally adjacent to the target win_chance = clamp( attack_power / (attack_power + defense_power), 5%, 90% )

Refunds

abandon refund = 50% of everything paid into the tile repossession refund = 30% of everything paid, after 30+ days inactive

Daily streak

reward = ₲150 × min(current streak, 7)

Offline earnings

if time since last sync ≤ 2 minutes: rent × (2 if boosted else 1) × elapsed seconds else: rent × 0.5 × min(elapsed seconds, 8 hours)

Landmark perks

build_speed / siege_discount / rush_discount: −2% per tile owned, capped at 30% (region-scoped) energy_boost: +2 energy/day per tile owned (account-wide, uncapped)

Covenant modifiers

A covenant applies as a multiplier on the relevant formula above, on that one tile — rent, def, atk_cost, build_time and build_cost each multiply the corresponding line, and max_level lowers what counts as "fully built" for that tile.

tile_rent = rent_formula × covenant_rent defense = defense_formula × covenant_def × region_project_defense attack_cost = attack_cost_formula × covenant_atk_cost (defender's covenant) build_secs = build_formula × covenant_build_time × region_project_build_time upgrade_cost = upgrade_formula × covenant_build_cost Ground Lease / The Black Ledger: after `expires_days` from signing, the card's `after` terms REPLACE its terms entirely (0% and 50% rent).

Region levy, upkeep & market duty

region_fund += 10% of each claim / build / rush spent in that region project_cost = ₲120,000 per project (2 active per region) upkeep = ₲40 per day, per active project, per tile you own there market_duty = 5% of sale proceeds, paid by the seller (waived by Free Port)

Region project effects

Metro Line: build_time × 0.75 Contractors' Guild: rush_cost × 0.60 Civic Fortifications: defense × 1.35 Free Port: market_duty waived within the region

Covenant offer odds

offered once per tile per tenure, on the first redevelop only (and only if the tile has no covenant and you haven't declined one) ~7% of offers: 1 rare deal + 2 standard cards otherwise: 3 standard cards, weighted-random from the eligible pool
